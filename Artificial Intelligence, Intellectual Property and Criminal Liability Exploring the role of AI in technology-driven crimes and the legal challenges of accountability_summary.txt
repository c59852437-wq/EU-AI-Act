1. Introduction
John McCarthy, who is regarded as ‘the father of Artificial intelligence’ has defined Artificial Intelligence(AI) as
‘the science and engineering of making intelligent machines, especially intelligent computer programs’1. In
other words, AI can be defined as the capability of a machine to imitate intelligent human behavior by way
of data that is feed into it.
AI can be categorized into two main types: narrow and general AI. Narrow AI which is also called weak AI
is the AI that is built to perform certain specific functions and which we use on daily basis, examples may
include voice assistant etc. General AI which is also known as strong AI can perform across various
domains and can outperform humans.2
With the rise of usage of AI worldwide, the AI is also increasingly being used in criminal activities like deep-
fake, cybercrime, hacking, digital arrest etc. Due to AI, it is difficult to trace criminal and attribute liability
which is a major challenge. Federal Bureau of Investigation of the USA in its 2025 report observed that
around 20 billion dollars were defrauded by using AI to commit cybercrimes in the USA.3 Reports also
suggest that AI- enabled ransomware attacks reached record levels in 2025-2026, which were used in
automated phishing attacks, identification of system vulnerabilities by automated AI attacks4. AI is also
widely used in deep-fake pornography targeting women, children.5
In a recent case of Ahmedabad, India, AI was used by the criminals in identity theft to manipulate Aadhar-
linked data. The accused generated fake facial blink videos from photographs to bypass KYC verification.
